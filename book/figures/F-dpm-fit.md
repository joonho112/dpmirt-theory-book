A Dirichlet process mixture and a Gaussian Rasch model, both item-centred, fitted to one simulated dataset of 400 persons and 25 items with a bimodal generating $G$. **This figure is about representational capacity, in the same sense as @fig-flexible-g: it shows what each prior *can* express, not how either performs.** No performance claim is made from it and none should be read out of it; the companion simulation study owns that question. **Left:** the generating density, the DPM posterior mean, and the Gaussian model's posterior mean. The DPM expresses two modes; the Gaussian family cannot express more than one at any parameter value. The shaded band is the pointwise 5th--95th percentile across posterior draws of the realized-partition mixture density — a posterior summary of that density, not a credible band for a Dirichlet-process functional. **Right:** the exact prior-predictive pmf of the occupied-cluster count $K$, obtained by integrating Antoniak's law over the $\mathrm{Gamma}(1,3)$ prior on $\alpha$, against its posterior. They differ by 0.36 in total variation: the prior places 0.32 on a single cluster and the posterior places 0.00. The data move $K$ substantially. Note also that $K$ is the occupied-partition count at this sample size, not the order of a true mixture, and @prp-partial-id concerns functionals of $G$ rather than either.
