**Equal quadratic-discrimination budgets do not conserve integrated information.** Two twenty-item tests share the same difficulties (normal quantiles) and $\sum_i \lambda_i^2=20$, equivalently the same summed item-peak budget $\sum_i \lambda_i^2/4=5$. One spreads that budget uniformly; the other assigns its strongest items to the centre of the population (shaded). But $\int \mathcal{J}(\theta)\,d\theta=\sum_i\lambda_i$, so the integrated information is 20.0000 for the uniform test and 18.0946 for the concentrated test, 9.53% lower. (a) The concentrated allocation nonetheless has a higher central peak and lower tails. (b) On the measurement-error scale, its tail deserts cost enough that its MSEM reliability $\bar\rho$ is lower despite the higher central peak.
