The risk of the James--Stein estimator @eq-james-stein relative to the risk $p$ of the usual estimator, computed from the expression James and Stein give in their § 2. Left: against $\|\xi\|^2$. The curve starts at $2/p$ — a hundredfold improvement at $p=200$ — and climbs toward 1, and the larger $p$ is the more slowly it climbs. Right: the same curves against $\|\xi\|^2/p$, the mean squared coordinate, which is the quantity that is comparable across dimensions; the dotted line marks a unit population variance. Read at $\|\xi\|^2/p = 1$ the gain is modest for $p=3$ and still substantial for $p=200$. Dimension determines the inadmissibility threshold, while both dimension and signal magnitude determine the size of the gain. Deterministic; no simulation. Generated by code/R/09-figures.R.
